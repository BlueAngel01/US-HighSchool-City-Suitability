### **2.1 Educational Resources**

Educational resources play an important role in students’ academic development and future opportunities. To evaluate the educational environment of each city, we selected four sub-indicators: higher education costs, high school graduation rate, student-teacher ratio, and library and research resources.

**(1) Higher Education Costs**

Higher education costs reflect the financial burden of pursuing further education. We use the average undergraduate tuition of selected universities within each city as the measurement. Lower tuition costs indicate better affordability and therefore receive higher scores.

[
 E_1=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_i}{n}
 ]

where (T_i) represents the undergraduate tuition of university (i), and (n) represents the number of selected universities.

**(2) High School Graduation Rate**

The high school graduation rate measures the overall educational performance of a city. A higher graduation rate indicates a better educational outcome.

[
 E_2=\frac{\text{Number of High School Graduates}}{\text{Total Number of High School Students}}\times100%
 ]

**(3) Student-Teacher Ratio**

The student-teacher ratio reflects the availability of teaching resources. A lower ratio generally indicates that students can receive more individual attention from teachers.

[
 E_3=\frac{\text{Total Number of Students}}{\text{Total Number of Teachers}}
 ]

**(4) Library and Research Resources**

Libraries and research facilities provide students with additional learning opportunities beyond the classroom. We measure library accessibility using the number of public libraries per 100,000 residents. A higher value indicates better access to educational resources.

[
 E_4=\frac{\text{Number of Public Libraries}}{\text{City Population}}\times100000
 ]

**Comprehensive Evaluation Model**

Since the four sub-indicators have different units and measurement scales, we first apply Min-Max Normalization to convert the raw data into standardized scores ranging from 0 to 100.

For positive indicators:

[
 S_i=\frac{X_i-X_{\min}}{X_{\max}-X_{\min}}\times100
 ]

For negative indicators:

[
 S_i=\frac{X_{\max}-X_i}{X_{\max}-X_{\min}}\times100
 ]

Finally, the overall Educational Resources Score is calculated using a weighted sum:

[
 \boxed{E=w_1S_1+w_2S_2+w_3S_3+w_4S_4}
 ]

where (w_i) represents the weight of each sub-indicator, determined through the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP), and:

[
 \sum_{i=1}^{4}w_i=1
 ]

A higher final score indicates a more favorable educational environment for high school students.
